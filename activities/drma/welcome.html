What is Digital Resilience?

Digital resilience is about more than surviving disruption — it is about continuing to deliver outcomes in the face of it. In today’s environment, organisations are challenged not only by cyberattacks, but also by system outages, supply chain shocks, and rapid changes in customer demand. True resilience requires the ability to anticipate these events, respond effectively when they occur, and adapt quickly so the business emerges stronger.

Industry leaders frame this in similar ways:

Splunk

Describes digital resilience as the ability for organisations to withstand, adapt to, and rapidly recover from digital disruptions, ensuring continuity of services while maintaining trust and compliance.

World Economic Forum

Defines it as an organisation’s capacity to anticipate, withstand, recover from, and evolve to address the impact of cyber incidents and digital shocks.

Together, these perspectives highlight that digital resilience is not a single control or tool, but a combination of technical, organisational, and human capabilities. Measuring and improving resilience across all of these dimensions is essential for long-term confidence and competitiveness.

The 8 Domains of Digital Resilience

Digital resilience is the ability to continue delivering outcomes when disruption occurs — whether from a cyberattack, system failure, or external shock. It is not a single control but a balanced set of qualities that determine how well an organisation can withstand, adapt, and recover.

The eight domains were chosen to cover the full spectrum of resilience. Some focus on the technical foundations of keeping systems available, performant, secure, and scalable. Others reflect the organisational capabilities that sustain resilience — efficiency, transparency, and adaptability. Finally, resilience depends on the human dimension — how intuitive systems are to use and how easily teams can operate them under pressure.

Measuring across all eight provides a clear picture of current strengths and gaps. This enables leaders to target investment where it delivers the greatest impact and to build resilience that is both robust in a crisis and sustainable over time.

The 5 Maturity Levels

Progress is cumulative. Higher levels build on the foundations of the earlier ones.

On the Overview page, choosing a level displays all practices up to that level.

Level 1 – Ad Hoc: Resilience practices are minimal, reactive, and inconsistent. Success depends on individual effort rather than process or design.

Level 2 – Repeatable: Basic practices exist and can be repeated, but they are informal, siloed, and not yet standardised across the organisation.

Level 3 – Defined: Resilience processes are documented, measured, and applied consistently. Responsibilities and expectations are clear.

Level 4 – Managed: Practices are monitored, tested, and increasingly automated. Performance is actively managed against resilience objectives.

Level 5 – Optimised: Resilience is embedded into culture with continuous improvement and predictive capabilities. The organisation adapts quickly and confidently to disruption.
